If you have looked into watching how your leads get handled, you have probably run into a confusing pile of names: decoy lead, seed lead, test lead, honeypot lead, planted lead. They sound like five different things. They are not. They are five names for one simple, old idea: a believable fake you place on purpose so you can see what happens to it.

This post explains what the tool actually is, why the names differ, and the two problems it solves better than anything else.

## One idea, many names

The vocabulary mostly depends on who is talking.

- **Decoy lead** and **honeypot lead** come from the fraud and security world, where a decoy or honeypot is something planted to attract and expose bad behavior.
- **Test lead** is what an operations or sales manager calls it: a lead whose only job is to test the process.
- **Planted lead** describes the action: you planted it.
- **Seed lead** is the term we use, because you seed it into a real flow and let it travel through. Our product name for one is a Dummy Lead.

Pick whichever word your team already uses. Underneath, the requirements are identical.

## What makes a planted lead actually work

A decoy that any rep or buyer can spot is worthless. They will simply handle the obvious test correctly and go back to their habits on everything else. For the tool to work, the fake has to be real in every way that a person handling it can check:

- **A real name and identity** that looks like an ordinary prospect.
- **A dedicated inbox** that can receive and keep every email, headers and all.
- **A live phone number** that genuinely rings, records calls, and receives texts.
- **A normal point of entry**, so it arrives the same way real leads do: the same form, the same feed, the same round-robin.

Get those right and the lead is indistinguishable from a genuine one to everybody except you. That is the whole game. The moment it is distinguishable, the experiment is contaminated.

## Problem one: catching resale of "exclusive" leads

This is where a planted lead has no real substitute. Suppose you buy leads sold to you as exclusive. Exclusive is supposed to mean the record goes to you and to no one else.

Place a seed lead into that buyer's feed. Because it is exclusive to a single recipient, there is exactly one party who should ever contact it: the buyer you placed it with. If a second, unrelated number starts calling or texting that seed, the record was passed on or sold. You are no longer guessing from a soft signal like "my prospects all sound shopped." You have a timestamp and a phone number. We go deep on this in [how to tell if your exclusive leads are being resold](/blog/exclusive-leads-being-resold/).

## Problem two: proving how leads get worked

The same planted lead measures handling quality, whether the handler is an outside buyer, a channel partner, or your own sales floor:

- **Speed to lead:** the real gap between arrival and first contact.
- **Attempts:** how many times someone actually tried, across call, text, and email.
- **Script and professionalism:** judged from the actual recording, not a self-report.

Because no one knows which lead is the test, you capture normal behavior instead of best behavior. That is exactly the logic behind running [a secret shopper for your sales leads](/blog/secret-shopper-for-sales-leads/).

## Build your own, or use a service

You can assemble a decoy lead by hand: register an identity, set up a mailbox, buy a phone line that can record, and then babysit all of it for the length of a follow-up sequence. It works, but it gets unwieldy fast, especially across more than one or two seeds, and the recordings and timelines live in five different places.

The alternative is a service that mints the identity, the inbox, and the live number for you, captures every touch in one timeline, and scores inbound calls for spam risk and attestation. That is what DummyLead does. Either way, the principle is the same one secret shoppers have used for decades: the only way to see how something is really handled is to watch it when no one knows you are watching.
